# Visual indicators of food spoilage

Spoilage is usually visible before it is dangerous, but the reverse is not true:
food can carry a hazardous load of pathogens while still looking and smelling
acceptable. Visual inspection is a screening tool, never a clearance test.

## Mould
Fuzzy or powdery growth in white, green, blue or black. On soft foods (bread,
soft fruit, yoghurt, jam, soft cheese) mould filaments penetrate far beyond the
visible patch, so the whole item is discarded. On dense low-moisture foods
(hard cheese, firm carrots) cutting 2.5 cm around and below a small patch is
accepted practice.

## Discolouration
Browning at cut surfaces is often enzymatic and harmless. Grey, green or
iridescent sheens on meat, and dark slimy patches on leafy greens, indicate
microbial growth rather than oxidation.

## Texture change
Slime on the surface of meat, fish or vegetables is a bacterial biofilm and is a
discard signal. Softening and weeping in fruit is degradation of the cell wall —
unappetising, and an entry point for microbes, but not on its own a pathogen.

## Packaging
A bulging lid, a swollen pouch or a hissing can indicates gas-producing
organisms and is an immediate, non-negotiable discard. Never taste-test it.
